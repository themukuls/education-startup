**The single sentence version:** ParentProof is not another tuition app — it is an
*independent audit* of the tuition you are already paying for, sold to Delhi NCR
parents of Class 8–10 CBSE children, primarily through WhatsApp, gated-society
ground events and tuition-centre partnerships, timed so that the campaign peaks
exactly when board-exam panic peaks.

**Why this 26-week window is the right one:** it ends on 25 Jan 2027, about three
weeks before CBSE boards begin (~14 Feb 2027). Indian parents' willingness to pay
for anything exam-related rises through December and January and collapses the day
boards start. We are selling into a rising tide the entire time, and we stop before
the tide goes out.

## 1. Positioning: the wedge

Every edtech in India sells the same promise: *"we will teach your child better."*
That market is crowded, expensive, and — after the BYJU'S collapse — distrusted.
Parents in NCR have been sold ₹1-lakh packages and watched them fail.

ParentProof sells something structurally different:

> **"You spend ₹60,000 a year on tuition. What is your receipt?"**

We are the **CA for your child's learning**. An accountant does not run your
business; they tell you the truth about it. That is the entire pitch, and it works
because:

1. **It is not competitive with tuition** — so tuition centres will partner with us
   instead of fighting us. (This unlocks the B2B2C channel, which is ~20% of the
   revenue plan.)
2. **It is credible post-BYJU'S** — "we won't teach your child, we'll audit what
   you're buying" is the opposite of the promise parents got burned on.
3. **It is cheap relative to the thing it audits** — ₹999 for six months against
   ₹5,000/month tuition is a 3% cost to verify a 100% spend. That framing closes
   the price objection before it opens.
4. **It produces proof** — the app locks a predicted board score *before* the exam
   and shows accuracy *after*. Nobody else in this market publishes receipts.

**Category name to use consistently:** *learning audit*. Never "test app,"
"practice app," or "edtech." Words create the frame.

## 2. Who exactly we are selling to

**Primary buyer — "Anxious Anjali"**
- Mother, 32–45, of a Class 8/9/10 CBSE child
- Household income ₹8–25 lakh; lives in a gated society in NCR
- Already pays ₹3,000–8,000/month for tuition and does not know if it works
- Is a node in 2–4 WhatsApp groups (class group, society group, mothers' group)
- Buys when a report card surprises her, or when boards get close

She is the target because she is **both the decision-maker and the distribution
channel.** One happy Anjali reaches forty parents for free.

**Secondary buyer — "ROI Rakesh"**
- Father, 35–50, pays the fees, thinks in spreadsheets
- The hook that works on him: *"You have a receipt for the fees. Where is the
  receipt for the learning?"*

**Not our customer (for now):** Class 6–7 parents (urgency too low), Class 11–12
(different exam system, JEE/NEET-dominated), non-CBSE boards (our syllabus corpus
is CBSE-first), and anyone outside NCR until the playbook is proven.

## 3. Channel strategy — and the one trap to avoid

**The trap:** BYJU'S died of customer acquisition cost. If the plan depends on paid
ads, the plan dies too. Indian K-12 edtech CPI benchmarks run ₹40–120 per *install*
— and installs are not payers. At a 60% activation rate and a 10% activation-to-paid
rate, a ₹90 install becomes a **₹1,500 paid CAC** before you blink.

**The defence:** paid ads carry **only 45%** of payers in this plan. The other 55%
comes from channels where CAC is labour, not cash:

| Channel | Share of payers | Why it works here | Cost shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ground events** (society melas, park tables, PTM gates) | ~18% | NCR families live in dense gated clusters. A tablet, a table and 20 minutes produces a finished audit a parent can hold. Highest-trust demo in the business. | ₹1.5–4k per event |
| **Tuition/school partners (B2B2C)** | ~20% of revenue | Centres need retention proof for their own parents. We give them a report with their name on it and ₹200–250 margin per child. They sell for us. | Rev-share only |
| **Referral** | ~12% | The WhatsApp class group *is* the distribution network. One month free per paying referral. | Deferred revenue |
| **Paid ads (Meta)** | ~45% | Scales, and is the only channel that scales on demand. Kept under a hard CAC guardrail. | ₹450–650/payer |
| **Organic/content/webinars/PR** | rest | Compounds. Free. Slow to start — which is why it starts in Week 1, not Week 12. | Time |

**The hard guardrail, written down so you don't argue with yourself later:**
> If blended paid CAC exceeds **₹650** in the first 13 weeks or **₹450** after that,
> cut the ad budget by half the same day and shift the money to ground events.
> No exceptions, no "let it run one more week."

## 4. The proof engine (the real moat)

This is the most important section in this document.

The app can lock a **predicted score before an exam** and reveal **accuracy after
results**. That converts an ordinary product feature into a marketing flywheel that
competitors cannot copy without also waiting a full exam cycle.

**How the calendar exploits it:**

| Moment | Date | What we do |
|---|---|---|
| Half-yearly exams | mid–late Sept | Get every user to lock a prediction (W8, ~20 pilots; W20, ~500 users) |
| Half-yearly results | late Sept (W9) | **Results Shock v1** — publish receipts, convert the surprised |
| Pre-boards | Dec (W20–21) | Lock predictions at scale |
| Pre-board results | early Jan (W24) | **Results Shock v2** — the single highest-converting week of the plan |
| Boards | ~14 Feb 2027 | Plan has already ended. Harvest complete. |

Every ad, every case study and every webinar after Week 9 leads with real receipts
instead of claims. This is why CAC *falls* through the plan while volume rises —
an unusual and very good shape.

**Non-negotiable:** publish accuracy honestly, including misses. A published miss
buys more trust than a hidden one, and the entire positioning is "independent."

## 5. The revenue math to ₹1 crore

Full model with editable assumptions: `03_Revenue_Funnel_Budget_Model.xlsx`.

| Line | Value |
|---|---|
| D2C paying families (26 weeks) | **7,195** |
| Blended D2C ARPU (80% Core ₹999 / 20% Annual ₹1,799) | **₹1,159** |
| D2C revenue | **₹83.4 L** |
| B2B2C children via partners | **2,840** @ ₹749 |
| B2B2C revenue | **₹21.3 L** |
| **Total** | **₹1,04,66,165 (₹1.047 Cr)** |
| Marketing budget | **₹22.6 L** (21.6% of revenue) |
| Blended CAC across all payers | **~₹226** |

**The ramp** (weekly D2C payers): 5 → 40 by W5 → 100 by W8 → 250 by W13 →
400 by W19 → 680 by W26. It roughly doubles every five weeks, which is what a
working referral loop plus a widening partner base plus rising seasonal intent
produce together.

### An honest reality check

**7,195 paying families from a standing start in 26 weeks is aggressive.** You
should go in knowing three things:

1. **The plan is designed to degrade gracefully.** See the *Scenarios & Levers* tab
   in file 03: at 0.6× execution it still lands **₹62.8 lakh**. A ₹63L year-one
   business built on a real moat is an excellent outcome, not a failure.
2. **The riskiest number is the ₹749 B2B2C channel** — it assumes 16+ signed
   partners by January. Partner deals are slow and personal. If by Week 13 you have
   fewer than 6 live partners, pull contingency lever #1 (one 500-child school deal
   at ₹599 = ₹3L in a single contract) rather than trying to make it up in ads.
3. **Weeks 1–5 will feel like failure.** You are targeting 85 payers in five weeks.
   That is correct and intentional — those five weeks buy the testimonials, the
   objection list and the ad copy that make weeks 6–26 possible. Do not panic-spend
   on ads in Week 3.

**The most likely honest outcome** if you execute well but not perfectly: ₹60–80
lakh, with a real referral engine and 10+ partners. Treat ₹1 Cr as the stretch that
sets the pace, not the definition of success.

## 6. The operating rhythm

**Every day**
- Reply to every inbound within **15 minutes** (this single habit is worth more than any ad)
- One WhatsApp Status post (6 value : 1 sell)
- Log every objection **verbatim** into `05 → Objection Handling`

**Every week**
- Mon: weekly review — funnel vs plan, kill/scale calls, one fix shipped
- Tue–Fri: acquisition + BD field work
- Sat: the big ground event or webinar (this is where volume lives)
- Sun: 1:1 conversion sprint — calls and voice notes to warm leads

**Every month**
- CAC by channel, cohort retention, refund rate (<2%), NPS (≥50)
- Kill anything with CAC > guardrail; double anything under it

**The one metric that matters:** paying families this week vs target. Everything
else is diagnosis.

## 8. Risk register

| Risk | Signal it's happening | Response |
|---|---|---|
| CAC spiral | Paid CAC > guardrail two weeks running | Halve ad budget same day; shift to ground/referral |
| Partner channel stalls | < 6 live partners by W13 | Contingency lever #1: one big school bulk deal |
| Activation drops | Install→first-test < 50% | Freeze acquisition for one week; fix onboarding only |
| Accuracy embarrassment | Predictions miss badly at W9 | Publish it honestly with the reason; pivot proof to chapter-gap discovery |
| Founder burnout | Missed weekly reviews | Non-negotiable Sunday half-day off; the plan assumes a functioning human |
| Support collapse at volume | Reply SLA > 2h | Hire the second intern early; automate FAQ replies |
| Product bug in peak week | Any Dec–Jan outage | Freeze all deploys W22–W26 except hotfixes |
