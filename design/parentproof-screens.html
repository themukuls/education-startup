02

The Test

The one fragile moment in the whole business — a 13-year-old actually taking a test his parent imposed. Design goal: make it feel short, fair, and low-stakes for the child, while quietly capturing whether each answer is memorized or understood. Ten minutes, hard cap.

Screen 05 · Handoff
A ritual, framed for the kid
  • The hand-off screen literally scripts the parent line — "aaj ka test de do" — turning a chore into a repeatable ritual.
  • Reassures the child: only 10 minutes, 9 questions. Low stakes reduces refusal (the #1 kill-risk).
  • Supervision is stated as normal, which also deters cheating without feeling like surveillance.

Screen 06 · Question
Where memorized ≠ understood is caught
  • Every question is tagged Recall / Understand / Apply — visible to the child as a subtle signal, and the core of the diagnosis.
  • This "Understand" item can't be beaten by rote — a kid who memorized steps but not why picks A. That's the exact signal parents pay for.
  • Timer + one-sitting + no back-Googling (distinct from a homework app) protects data quality.

How these 14 screens hang together. The design has one job on every screen: make an independent audit feel credible and calm, never like edtech. Serif documents + a verification stamp carry authority; a strict readiness traffic-system keeps status honest; and the marigold seal is spent only on brand and proof — never on decoration.

The spine

Onboard & consent → child takes a 10-min test → parent gets a stamped diagnosis + fix → the year-long ledger → verify the prediction → convert. Every loop feeds the moat.

Designed against the pre-mortem

Kid refusal (streaks, 10-min cap), anxiety-without-agency (fix always attached), prediction misses (ranges), and DPDP (consent-first) each have a screen answering them.

Not built, on purpose

No live classes, no gamified kid universe, no school-facing dashboard. The child sees only a test; the parent owns everything. Independence is the product.
